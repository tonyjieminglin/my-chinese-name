Our approach

Thoughtful naming,
not instant translation.

A good Chinese name is a small piece of language you may use for years. We take the time to explain what it says, how it sounds and why the characters belong together.

The method

Three lenses.
One considered name.

Sound and pronunciation

We begin with the name you use and how you would like it to sound. Chinese syllables and tones do not map neatly onto English sounds, so a natural result may be an adaptation rather than a literal transliteration.

Character meaning

Each character has its own meaning, history and associations. We consider the characters together, not as isolated dictionary entries.

Naturalness and context

We look at whether the combination feels plausible as a Chinese personal name, whether its associations are appropriate, and whether it matches your preferred style.

What your delivery includes

Understand the name

Your name should come with an explanation you can actually use—not just two characters on a page.

Characters & pinyin

The Chinese characters, standard Hanyu Pinyin and tone marks.

Pronunciation notes

A plain-English guide to the syllables and tones, with the limits of English approximations explained.

Meaning & context

A careful explanation of the characters, their combined impression and any relevant cultural nuance.

What we do not promise

We do not claim that a name predicts destiny, changes fortune or guarantees success. We do not present a name as a legal identity document. Our service is a cultural and linguistic naming experience, not legal, genealogical or fortune-telling advice.

Chinese naming conventions vary across regions, families and communities. Where a choice is subjective, we will say so rather than present one interpretation as universal.
